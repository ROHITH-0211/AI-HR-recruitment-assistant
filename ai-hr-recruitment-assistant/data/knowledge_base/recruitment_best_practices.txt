RECRUITMENT BEST PRACTICES

Fair and Unbiased Screening
- Evaluate candidates strictly on skills, experience, qualifications, projects, and certifications.
- Do not factor gender, race, religion, age, disability, nationality, sexual orientation, political
  affiliation, or any other protected or sensitive characteristic into any score or recommendation.
- If such information appears in a resume, ignore it entirely during evaluation.
- Use structured, consistent criteria across all candidates for the same role so decisions are explainable.

Match Scoring Philosophy
- Required skills should carry more weight than preferred/nice-to-have skills.
- A strong overall project/experience narrative can offset a few missing preferred skills.
- Experience "years" is a rough proxy — depth and relevance of experience matters more than raw tenure.
- Always explain why a score was given in plain language, referencing specific skills and experience.

Recommendation Categories
- Strongly Recommend: meets or exceeds nearly all required skills and experience; strong overall fit.
- Recommend: meets most required skills; minor, learnable gaps; solid overall fit.
- Consider: meets some required skills but has notable gaps; may be worth an exploratory interview.
- Not Recommended: missing multiple critical required skills or experience level; poor overall fit.

Responsible Use of AI in Hiring
- AI-generated match scores and recommendations are decision-support only, not a final hiring decision.
- A human recruiter or hiring manager should always review AI output before any hiring action is taken.
- Clearly label AI-assisted output as such to every user of the system.

Candidate Experience
- Keep the process transparent: candidates should understand what is being evaluated and why.
- Provide constructive, specific feedback wherever possible instead of generic rejections.
